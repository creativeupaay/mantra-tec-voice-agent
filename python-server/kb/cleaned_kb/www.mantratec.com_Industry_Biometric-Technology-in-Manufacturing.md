## The Problem - Buddy Punching Costs More Than Payroll

In a 500-worker plant, even a 3% rate of buddy punching - one worker clocking in for another - translates to thousands of lost productive hours monthly and payroll leakage that goes undetected in paper or PIN-based systems. The financial loss is significant, but the operational consequences run deeper: labour compliance records are inaccurate, overtime calculations are unreliable, and workforce analytics are built on flawed data.

Beyond payroll, unverified access to restricted zones creates safety and compliance risks that carry legal exposure. An uncertified operator in a high-voltage area, a chemical handling zone, or a machinery bay is a liability - and a paper sign-in register cannot prevent it from happening.

Mantra fingerprint terminals tie every clock-in and clock-out to a unique biological identity. There is no card to lend, no PIN to share, and no way to register attendance for someone who is not physically present. Zone access is enforced the same way - a worker can only enter areas they are trained, certified, and authorised to enter. The result is payroll accuracy, zone safety, and labour compliance records that are defensible in any audit.

## FAQ - Frequently Asked Questions

Questions plant managers and HR directors ask before deploying biometric attendance in manufacturing.

### How does fingerprint attendance completely eliminate buddy punching?

Buddy punching is only possible when attendance can be recorded by something transferable - a card, a PIN, a physical token. Fingerprints cannot be transferred. The biometric that must be present at the terminal to record an attendance event belongs exclusively to one person and cannot be replicated. Even if a worker wanted to clock in for an absent colleague, they would need to present that colleague's actual finger - which is impossible in practice. The elimination of buddy punching is total, not partial, when biometric attendance replaces card or PIN systems.

### What happens when a worker's fingerprint cannot be read due to calluses or oil?

Workers with heavily callused, oil-covered, or damaged fingertips can use face recognition as a primary or fallback authentication method - without removing gloves or PPE. Mantra terminals support simultaneous fingerprint and face recognition enrollment, so both modalities are available for each worker. The system automatically falls back to face recognition when fingerprint quality falls below the acceptance threshold. Workers who are unable to enroll a quality fingerprint from any finger - a small minority even in industrial settings - can be enrolled exclusively on face recognition from the start.

### How is zone access control set up for workers with different certifications?

Zone access is configured through a central administration panel where each zone in the plant is defined with its required certification level, role, and shift parameters. Each worker is assigned to the zones they are certified and authorised to enter. When a worker presents their biometric at a zone terminal, the system checks their profile against the zone's requirements in real time. If they are not authorised, the door remains locked and the attempt is logged. Zone access matrices can be updated instantly from the central panel - useful when certifications expire or roles change.

### What happens to attendance recording when the network goes down?

Mantra terminals operate in standalone mode when network connectivity is lost. The worker templates and access control rules stored locally on each terminal continue to function, enabling attendance recording and zone access control to continue uninterrupted. All transactions recorded in offline mode are queued in local memory and automatically uploaded to the central server when the network is restored. No attendance events are lost during network outages, and the integrity of the audit trail is maintained throughout.
