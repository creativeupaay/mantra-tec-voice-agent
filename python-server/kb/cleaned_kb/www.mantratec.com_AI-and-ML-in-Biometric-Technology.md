## AI Is Changing Identity Verification

Mantra's biometric systems use machine learning models that evaluate patterns probabilistically rather than relying on rigid match criteria.

### Deepfakes Meet Measured Analysis

High-resolution face swaps and synthetic overlays are no longer experimental. They are accessible, automated, and increasingly realistic. Mantra's convolutional neural network models evaluate texture gradients, sub-surface skin scattering, micro-reflection inconsistencies, and depth variance during capture. By analyzing spatial and temporal inconsistencies that synthetic media struggles to replicate, the system distinguishes presentation attacks from genuine capture attempts in real time.

### Liveness Detection at the Device Level

Presentation attacks are most effective when detection happens too late. Mantra implements ISO/IEC 30107-compliant Presentation Attack Detection directly on the device. This means spoof indicators such as silicone overlays, printed irises, high-resolution screen replays, or molded fingerprints are evaluated at the point of capture. Decisions are made within milliseconds, before data is transmitted or matched. Running PAD locally reduces latency, minimizes exposure risk, and prevents compromised inputs from entering backend workflows.

### Adaptive Models That Learn From Deployment

Threat patterns do not remain static. A spoofing method that bypasses controls today will evolve tomorrow. Mantra's machine learning models are periodically retrained on new datasets reflecting emerging attack vectors and environmental variables. As deployments scale, the system benefits from exposure to broader biometric variation and attempted bypass techniques. Performance improvements are driven by model refinement and dataset expansion, not just firmware updates. This approach supports long-term programs where accuracy must remain stable across years of usage and millions of transactions.

### Multimodal Confidence Scoring

No single biometric modality is infallible in every condition. Lighting, sensor wear, environmental interference, or physiological changes can introduce ambiguity. Mantra's fusion engine assigns weighted confidence scores across modalities. If facial recognition confidence drops due to lighting or obstruction, the system can incorporate iris, fingerprint, or behavioral signals to strengthen the decision. Authentication is based on aggregated risk assessment rather than isolated matches. This layered scoring model reduces false rejections while maintaining strong resistance to spoofing attempts.
